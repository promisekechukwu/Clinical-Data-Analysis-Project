# Clinical-Data-Analysis-Project: Comparing Kidney Function Between Male and Female Adult Patients
### Kidney Function &amp; Biomarker Gender Analysis is a healthcare data analysis project that investigates sex-based differences in renal filtration (eGFR) and metabolic waste accumulation (Serum Creatinine) among adult Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) patients.
### I recently wrapped up a healthcare data analysis project examining sex-based differences in renal biomarkers—specifically Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) and Serum Creatinine (Cr)—across Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) stages in an adult clinical cohort ($N = 1,138$). In clinical analytics, raw lab values can often be misleading if we do not account for underlying physiological and demographic variations, making this an ideal deep dive into real-world health data.
### What I Did :### Data Cleaning & Preparation: Ingested the raw clinical records from ROUTE_proteinuria_dataset.csv, audited and removed duplicate entries, systematically dropped 45 non-essential clinical variables, and isolated adult patients ($\text{age} \ge 18$; comprising 792 males and 346 females) to establish a clean, reliable baseline cohort.
### Statistical Analysis: Evaluated central tendencies (means, medians, standard deviations) and stage-wise biomarker distributions across CKD Stages 2 through 5, comparing male and female subgroups across filtration and waste metrics.
### Custom Visualizations: Built multi-panel dashboards using pure Python (Pandas, NumPy, and Matplotlib)—strictly avoiding high-level wrapper libraries to maintain granular control—to visualize biomarker distributions, demographic histograms, stage proportions, and physiological scatter curves. 
## Key Findings & Clinical Takeaways 
### The Creatinine–eGFR Paradox: Male patients showed consistently higher mean serum creatinine levels across every CKD stage ($2.33\text{ mg/dL}$ overall vs. $2.14\text{ mg/dL}$ in females), yet maintained slightly higher or comparable eGFR levels ($33.58$ vs. $30.73\text{ mL/min/1.73m}^2$). This reflects higher baseline skeletal muscle mass and daily creatine turnover in males rather than genuinely better renal filtration capacity.
### Exponential Waste Accumulation: Mapping serum creatinine against eGFR highlighted a sharp curvilinear inverse relationship—once eGFR drops below $30\text{ mL/min/1.73m}^2$ (Stages 4 and 5), minor losses in kidney filtration cause steep, exponential spikes in blood creatinine levels.
### Stage Progression Disparities: Male patients concentrated heavily in moderate disease (Stage 3, at $45.58\%$ vs. $31.50\%$ of females), while a higher proportion of female patients presented in end-stage impairment (Stage 5, at $26.01\%$ vs. $15.03\%$ of males), underscoring the critical importance of muscle-adjusted eGFR formulas for accurate early clinical detection.
